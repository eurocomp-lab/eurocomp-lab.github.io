Survey questions and outcome indicators

Survey question repository

Before writing a single question, review existing, validated survey instruments. Using questions that have already been tested in comparable evaluations saves time, improves data quality, and makes findings comparable with evidence from other programmes and regions. Key reference sources include:

  • Community Innovation Survey (CIS) - the standard EU framework for measuring innovation activity in firms
  • Firms Adoption of Technology Survey (FAT) - World Bank instrument on technology adoption across sectors
  • World Bank Enterprise Surveys (WBES) - firm-level outcomes including investment, productivity, and the business environment
  • World Management Survey (WMS) - management practices across firms and countries
  • Flash Eurobarometer on SME Digitalisation - digital technology adoption and skills across EU member states
  • ECB Survey on Access to Finance of Enterprises (SAFE) - financing conditions and constraints for SMEs

The survey question repository brings together questions drawn from the resources above, organised by topic. The questions are suggestions and a starting point, not a complete or prescribed questionnaire: they do not cover every topic an evaluation may need, and they do not have to be used exactly as written. The source surveys linked above give more detail and many more questions, so it is worth consulting them directly when a topic is central to your evaluation. Navigate to the theme most relevant to your evaluation, browse the questions and response options (where applicable), and select those that match the indicators in your Theory of Change. Then adapt the wording, response options, units and reference periods to your programme and national context.

RCT outcome repository

Indicators rarely need to be built from scratch. Managing Authorities should draw on existing, well-tested measures from comparable evaluations before designing new ones. The selection of outcomes and their definitions in this repository was informed by the EuroCOMP Lab evaluations (see Examples), policy reports such as the World Bank’s TIDES report, and established indicator manuals such as the OECD Oslo Manual for innovation and the OECD Frascati Manual for research and development. Drawing on these sources improves the quality of your data and makes findings easier to compare with evidence from other programmes and regions.

The RCT outcome repository provides a curated list of outcome indicators, organised by topic. The outcomes are suggestions and a starting point, not a complete or prescribed set: they do not cover every outcome an evaluation may need, and not all of them will suit every evaluation. Select the outcomes that match the instrument you are evaluating and the causal chain in your Theory of Change, and adapt the definitions and metrics to your programme and context. The reports and manuals above give more detail and further indicators, so it is worth consulting them directly when an outcome is central to your evaluation. For each outcome you will find:

  • The outcome name and a plain-language definition
  • The metric - how to operationalise and measure it
