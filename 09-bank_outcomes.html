Indicator repository

Indicators rarely need to be built from scratch. Managing authorities should draw on existing, well-tested measures from comparable evaluations before designing new ones. This repository provides a curated list of indicators, organised by topic, each with a plain-language definition and a metric that says how to operationalise and measure it.

Topics

Firm performance and growth

Turnover, profits, value added, productivity, wages, exports and firm survival.

Example: the TIDES of Change report (World Bank, 2025)

Innovation and intellectual property

Types and novelty of innovation, sales from new products, innovation spending, technology readiness, patents, trademarks and licensing.

Example: the returns to innovation subsidies in Romania (South-Muntenia)

R&D activity

Whether the firm does R&D, how much it spends and how many staff work on it.

Example: financial support to innovate in Spain (Extremadura)

Digitalisation and technology adoption

Technology adoption and spending, online sales, digital investment and skills, and digital maturity.

Example: financial support to digitalise in Romania (North-East)

Collaboration and knowledge transfer

Collaboration, partner diversity, links with universities and researchers, and cluster or network membership.

Example: matching businesses with scientists in Croatia

Employment and human capital

Jobs, job growth, high-skilled staff, training and wages.

Example: financial support to digitalise in Romania (North-East)

Business finance

Access to equity, debt and public finance, and its use for R&D and innovation.

Example: making firms investment-ready in the Western Balkans (2018)

Business support and training

Applications to, participation in and type of business support, and formal employee training.

Example: scaling up management consulting in Colombia

Management practices

An overall management index and its eight dimensions, from problem solving to dealing with underperformance.

Example: how management practices differ in the EU-27 (2025)

Energy efficiency

Energy use, cost and mix, on-site renewables, energy management, audits, investment and savings.

Example: grants for SME energy efficiency in Europe

How to use the indicators

The indicators are suggestions and a starting point, not a complete or prescribed set: they do not cover every indicator an evaluation may need, and not all of them will suit every evaluation.

  1. Open the topic that matches the intervention you are evaluating.
  2. Select the indicators that match the causal chain in your Theory of Change.
  3. Adapt the definitions and metrics to your intervention and context.
  4. Find tested questions to measure them in the survey question repository.

The reports and manuals below give more detail and further indicators, so it is worth consulting them directly when an indicator is central to your evaluation.

Sources

The selection of indicators and their definitions in this repository was informed by the EuroCOMP Lab evaluations (see Examples), policy reports such as the World Bank’s TIDES report, and established indicator manuals such as the OECD Oslo Manual for innovation and the OECD Frascati Manual for research and development. Drawing on these sources improves the quality of your data and makes findings easier to compare with evidence from other interventions and regions.
